The Anatomy of a Phishing Attack

A phishing attack is rarely a single event. It follows a repeatable sequence that security researchers call the phishing kill chain. Understanding each stage helps defenders identify where to intervene.

  1. Reconnaissance — The attacker gathers names, roles, and email addresses from public sources (LinkedIn, company websites, data breaches).
  2. Weaponization — A convincing message is crafted, often cloning a real brand's template and using a spoofed or look-alike domain.
  3. Delivery — The email, SMS, or call is sent. Attackers time delivery to maximize urgency (e.g., end of day, payroll week).
  4. Exploitation — The victim clicks a link, opens an attachment, or provides information over the phone.
  5. Installation / Action — Malware is installed, credentials are captured, or a fraudulent payment is initiated.
  6. Command & Control — The attacker maintains access, exfiltrates data, or monetizes the compromise.

Psychological Triggers

Social engineering succeeds because it exploits predictable human tendencies. The most common triggers are:

  • Authority — Messages appear to come from a CEO, IT department, or government agency.
  • Urgency — "Your account will be locked in 24 hours."
  • Fear — Threats of legal action, fines, or account loss.
  • Curiosity — "See who viewed your profile."
  • Reward — Fake refunds, bonuses, or prize notifications.
  • Helpfulness — Requests framed as a small favor from a "colleague."
