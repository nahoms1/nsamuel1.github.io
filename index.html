Abstract

Phishing and social engineering remain among the most successful and costly attack vectors in modern cybersecurity. Rather than exploiting software vulnerabilities, these attacks target the human element — manipulating trust, urgency, and authority to trick users into revealing credentials, transferring funds, or installing malware. This site examines how phishing attacks work, the psychological principles they exploit, and the technical and human defenses that organizations can deploy to reduce risk.

Key Terms

  • Phishing — fraudulent messages that impersonate a trusted party to steal information.
  • Spear Phishing — a targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific individual or role.
  • Business Email Compromise (BEC) — impersonation of an executive or vendor to trigger fraudulent payments.
  • Pretexting — inventing a believable scenario to justify a request for information.
  • MFA — Multi-Factor Authentication; a second verification step beyond a password.
  • Spoofing — falsifying an email address, domain, or caller ID to appear legitimate.
